Readyt's AI Reply Engine monitors Reddit around the clock and surfaces the threads most likely to convert — so you spend your time approving replies, not hunting for opportunities. Every reply Readyt drafts is written to match the subreddit's tone and your brand voice, making it indistinguishable from a genuine community response. Whether you review each reply manually or let auto-post handle the queue, the engine keeps your brand in the right conversations without the manual grind.

## How it works

The Reply Engine runs a continuous pipeline from signal detection all the way through to a live Reddit post.

**Scan.** Readyt connects to Reddit's data stream via websockets, pulling new threads and comments in real time. It monitors your configured keywords, competitor brand names, and subreddits that match your ICP — so nothing relevant slips through during off-hours.

**Score.** Each thread the engine finds is passed through Readyt's intent scoring pipeline (covered in detail below). The score determines whether a thread surfaces in your dashboard at all. Low-intent noise stays filtered out.

**Draft.** For every thread that clears the intent threshold, the engine generates a reply. It reasons against the full thread context, your brand voice settings, and the specific subreddit's community norms to produce a reply that reads like it came from a real, knowledgeable community member.

**Approve.** You review the draft in your dashboard alongside the intent score and thread context. Edit the copy if you want to, or approve it as-is with one click.

**Post.** Readyt posts the reply from your warmed Reddit account. The thread, reply, and any downstream attribution data are immediately tracked in Analytics.

## Intent scoring \{#intent-scoring\}

Not every Reddit mention is worth your time. Someone venting about a bad experience is different from someone actively asking for a tool recommendation. The intent scoring pipeline separates the two.

Every thread the engine surfaces is evaluated by an LLM pipeline that weighs the following signals:

- **Keyword match quality** — how closely the thread's language aligns with your target use case, not just a surface-level keyword hit
- **Competitor mentions** — threads where a competitor is named carry elevated intent, because the user is already shopping in your category
- **ICP fit** — the subreddit, poster history, and thread context are assessed against your defined ideal customer profile
- **Buying language** — phrases like "looking for," "switched from," "anyone recommend," and "worth it?" indicate active evaluation

The resulting score (0–100) appears next to every thread in your dashboard. Use it to prioritize: focus your manual review time on threads scoring above 70, and set auto-post thresholds for high-confidence matches.

## Drafting a reply

Readyt generates each reply against three inputs: the full thread content (including prior comments), your brand voice configuration, and the subreddit's community tone. The engine never produces a reply that starts with "As an AI" or reads like a press release — it reasons about what a helpful, credible community member would actually say.

You can steer tone on a per-reply or account-wide basis using three presets:

- **Helpful** — educational, empathetic, focused on solving the poster's problem
- **Direct** — confident, concise, no-fluff recommendation style
- **Story** — first-person framing, light narrative, works well in subreddits that favor personal experience

  </Step>

## Auto-post mode

Auto-post mode removes the approval step entirely. When enabled, Readyt posts replies automatically for any thread that meets your configured intent score threshold — no dashboard visit required.

This is the fastest way to operate at scale, but it works best when two conditions are met: your warmed account has a healthy karma score, and you've already reviewed enough manual replies to be confident in Readyt's voice calibration for your brand.

Turn on auto-post from **Settings → Reply Engine → Posting mode**. You can set separate thresholds for different subreddits or keyword groups, so high-confidence matches post automatically while borderline threads still route to your queue for review.

<Tip>
  Most users spend 15 minutes per day approving replies. Start in manual approval mode to tune your brand voice before switching to auto-post — a few days of manual review gives the engine enough feedback to calibrate confidently.
</Tip>
